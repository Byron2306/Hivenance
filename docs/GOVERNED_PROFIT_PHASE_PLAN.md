# Governed Profit Phase Plan

## Purpose

This document defines a practical phase plan for turning Hivenance from a multi-agent research system into a governed profit engine.

The core premise is:

1. Research does not directly earn trust.
2. Governance allocates trust only after forward evidence is earned.
3. Capital is a privilege granted to thesis families that survive truth-settlement, cost adjustment, and regime review.
4. Profit comes from selective permissioning, not from maximum activity.


## North Star

Hivenance should behave like an internal edge marketplace:

- workers propose ideas
- native models score them
- federated models challenge them
- baselines punish weak claims
- governance allocates trust
- capital follows only repeatedly validated edges

The system should optimize for:

- high-quality abstention
- controlled promotion
- regime-aware capital allocation
- rapid demotion after drift
- evidence-backed scaling


## System Thesis

The strongest unit of decision-making is not a single model or a single symbol.

The strongest unit is:

`thesis family x regime x symbol class`

Examples:

- breakout continuation in expansion regimes
- exhaustion mean reversion in stretched, illiquid spikes
- microstructure imbalance in high-depth, low-spread environments
- context consensus models during stable directional drift

This means the system should not ask:

- "Is model A good?"

It should ask:

- "Under which market conditions does model A earn permission to consume capital?"

## ~~Phase 0: Constitution and Authority Lock~~

### Objective

Create a system that cannot accidentally "profit-seek" by bypassing safety.

### Required capabilities

- explicit authority map
- clear separation between research, execution, promotion, and scaling authority
- protected actions locked behind dedicated phases
- deny-by-default posture for live capital changes
- durable event logging and audit trails

### Profit relevance

Profit systems die from unauthorized behavior faster than from weak signal quality.
This phase prevents accidental overreach and establishes trust boundaries.

### Exit criteria

- no research component can place real orders
- no integration sidecar can gain wallet authority
- all protected actions are explicitly vetoed outside approved phases
- every critical action path produces an auditable record


## ~~Phase 1: Observation and Market Truth Capture~~

### Objective

Build a reliable public-data truth spine for later profit claims.

### Required capabilities

- exchange observation loops
- candidate ranking by liquidity, spread, data freshness, and depth
- feature vector generation
- regime tagging inputs
- durable observation snapshots

### Profit relevance

This phase does not create profit directly. It creates the conditions under which profit claims can later be falsified.

Bad observation quality creates fake edge.
Good observation quality makes later model comparisons meaningful.

### Key metrics

- data quality
- freshness
- continuity ratio
- spread stability
- symbol eligibility rate
- observation success ratio

### Exit criteria

- observation quality consistently exceeds minimum thresholds
- feature vectors are stable enough for forward settlement
- symbol universe is filtered to tradable, liquid candidates


## ~~Phase 2: Hypothesis Competition~~

### Objective

Turn market observations into a research tournament.

### Required capabilities

- multiple native models
- deterministic baselines
- federated research-only challenger models
- multi-horizon forecasting
- immutable forecast logging before outcomes exist
- truth-settlement after horizon expiry

### Profit relevance

This is the first phase that can discover edge.
However, no model is trusted yet.

The goal is to determine:

- which ideas beat baselines
- which ideas only look good before costs
- which ideas are regime-sensitive
- which ideas are mostly abstention engines

### Governance role

Governance should not promote models globally here.
It should create evidence partitions:

- by model family
- by regime
- by symbol
- by horizon

### Key metrics

- mean net bps after cost
- action rate
- abstention rate
- win rate
- calibration quality
- edge stability over time
- baseline-relative outperformance

### Exit criteria

- at least one non-baseline thesis family is profitable after cost
- primary/federated models beat baselines in specific conditions
- no execution wiring contamination exists

## Phase 5: Shadow Governance and Human Review

### Objective

Let the system act as if it is live, while still withholding capital authority.

### Required capabilities

- shadow intents
- shadow fills
- shadow exits
- review workflows
- operator approval checkpoints
- parameter freeze requirements

### Profit relevance

This phase tests the social and operational discipline of the system.

The research stack may be strong, but if governance cannot review, reject, or pause responsibly, profit will not scale safely.

### Governance role

Governance should now evaluate:

- whether thesis families behave as expected in live flow
- whether confidence estimates remain sane
- whether human reviewers agree with automatic promotion suggestions

### Key metrics

- shadow fill ratio
- shadow PnL quality
- operator rejection rate
- model drift indicators
- decision latency

### Exit criteria

- shadow performance is consistent with validated expectations
- human review rejects bad promotions before they become costly
- parameter discipline is proven


## Phase 6: Tiny Live Capital with Slashing

### Objective

Introduce minimal real capital under strict stake-and-slash rules.

### Required capabilities

- tiny live canary execution
- hard notional caps
- deadman controls
- isolated account rules
- stake lock / release / slash accounting
- immediate halt on policy violations

### Profit relevance

This is where the system begins proving whether its evidence survives reality.

The first profit goal is not scale.
It is:

- clean live survivability
- low-regret execution
- honest attribution

### Governance role

Governance should allocate live permission only to thesis families that:

- passed shadow review
- retain edge after execution realism
- show acceptable drawdown and rejection behavior

Workers or thesis families should be slashable for:

- repeated low-quality recommendations
- regime misuse
- stale confidence
- violating risk budget assumptions

### Key metrics

- live realized net bps
- approval-to-execution conversion
- slashing frequency
- halt frequency
- variance versus shadow expectations

### Exit criteria

- tiny live capital is profitable or acceptably near break-even under very small size
- loss behavior is contained
- governance reacts correctly to degradation


## Phase 7: Regime-Aware Capital Routing

### Objective

Route capital according to evidence quality, regime fit, and current trust score.

### Required capabilities

- capital stage framework
- regime-conditioned trust scoring
- thesis family budget allocation
- automatic demotion and cooldown
- cross-family competition for scarce capital

### Profit relevance

This is where the system starts becoming clever.

Instead of asking:

- "Which model is best overall?"

It asks:

- "Which thesis family deserves capital in this regime right now?"

### Governance role

Governance should allocate:

- observation budget
- simulation budget
- shadow budget
- live notional budget

based on:

- recent forward evidence
- regime-specific quality
- drawdown state
- confidence decay

### Key metrics

- profit by thesis family x regime
- capital efficiency
- trust score drift
- regime misallocation cost
- cooldown recovery quality

### Exit criteria

- capital allocation is clearly better than flat allocation
- the system demonstrates profitable selectivity across regimes


## Phase 8: Thesis Market and Internal Edge Exchange

### Objective

Transform the platform into an internal marketplace where ideas compete for trust and budget.

### Required capabilities

- explicit thesis registry
- thesis family scorecards
- internal auction or ranking for scarce capital
- symbol- and regime-specific budget slots
- sidecar research ingestion as challenger evidence

### Profit relevance

This phase creates research diversity without losing governance discipline.

External systems such as:

- Freqtrade
- Jesse
- Hummingbot-derived logic
- transparent ML candidates

should not gain authority directly.
They should submit evidence into the thesis market.

### Governance role

Governance acts like a portfolio allocator across competing internal strategies.

It should reward:

- diversity with evidence
- orthogonal edge
- stable post-cost performance

It should punish:

- correlation masquerading as diversity
- overfit challenger models
- fake precision without edge

### Exit criteria

- multiple thesis families can coexist profitably without governance confusion
- challenger research improves allocation quality


## Phase 9: Autonomous Profit Governance

### Objective

Allow bounded autonomous portfolio adaptation without surrendering constitutional safety.

### Required capabilities

- trust decay engine
- self-demotion logic
- dynamic capital throttles
- bounded autonomous reallocations
- operator-only emergency override

### Profit relevance

This phase is about scaling decision quality, not just trade count.

The system should autonomously:

- reduce trust when edge weakens
- reallocate budget toward stronger thesis families
- increase abstention during uncertain regimes
- throttle exposure after correlated misses

### Governance role

Governance becomes a dynamic allocator of:

- trust
- attention
- simulation budget
- live budget

while still respecting human-owned emergency authority.

### Exit criteria

- autonomous reallocations improve risk-adjusted outcomes
- safety invariants remain intact
- capital changes remain explainable after the fact


## Cross-Phase Control Layer

These mechanisms should exist across all phases:

### 1. Stake and slash

Every meaningful recommendation engine should accumulate:

- earned trust
- stake-at-risk
- slash history
- recovery history

This converts "model performance" into governance memory.

### 2. Regime memory

The system should maintain long-lived evidence on:

- what works in expansion
- what fails in chop
- what degrades in hostile liquidity
- what only wins in rare dislocations

### 3. Promotion ladder

Every thesis family should progress through:

1. observed
2. hypothesized
3. simulated
4. validated
5. shadowed
6. canary-live
7. capital-routed

No skipping.

### 4. Demotion ladder

Every thesis family should also have a reverse ladder:

1. capital reduced
2. canary-only
3. shadow-only
4. research-only
5. quarantined


## What Actually Creates Profit

The actual edge is likely to come from five behaviors:

### 1. Better abstention

Most systems overtrade.
Hivenance should profit by refusing low-quality opportunities.

### 2. Better regime matching

A model that is mediocre globally may be excellent in one regime.
Governance should exploit this.

### 3. Faster demotion

Losing systems often fail because they hold trust too long.
Hivenance should remove trust aggressively after evidence decay.

### 4. Cross-family disagreement as signal

Disagreement is often informative.

Examples:

- momentum model bullish but microstructure model weak
- context model aligned but breakout model abstains
- native model acts, challengers abstain

Governance should treat disagreement as a capital-sizing input.

### 5. Capital as a reward, not a default

Capital should flow only after evidence earns it.
That simple rule is the system's core advantage.


## Immediate Next-Build Priorities

These are the highest-value next steps from the current system state.

### Priority A: Thesis family governance

Add scorecards and trust memory for:

- breakout
- reversion
- microstructure
- context consensus
- transparent ML proxies

### Priority B: Regime-conditioned evaluation

Extend scorecards to segment outcomes by regime.

Current evaluations are useful, but the real allocator should ask:

- profitable where?
- stable when?
- weak under what market state?

### Priority C: Stake/slash accounting at thesis-family level

Current governance ideas should map to explicit stake units.

Recommended first unit:

- stake per thesis family per regime bucket

### Priority D: Capital routing layer

Create a small allocator that chooses:

- no capital
- canary capital
- limited sleeve capital

based on:

- recent post-cost evidence
- slashing history
- regime fit
- drawdown state

### Priority E: Sidecar evidence normalization

Turn sidecar systems into structured challenger evidence.

They should contribute:

- backtest bundles
- export metrics
- strategy diagnostics
- parameter behavior clues

They should not contribute:

- autonomous live execution
- wallet authority
- direct capital routing


## Honest Risks

### Research illusion risk

Many federated models are transparent proxies, not true external runtimes.
Useful for diversity, but not magic.

### Governance theater risk

If stake/slash does not change real capital permission, governance becomes decorative.

### Complexity tax

Too many agents without clear capital consequences can reduce clarity faster than they add edge.

### Regime classifier fragility

If regime tagging is weak, capital routing may amplify bad decisions.

### Correlated false diversity

Multiple models may appear independent while actually reflecting the same underlying market feature.


## Final Recommendation

The best long-term premise is:

Hivenance should not be a trading bot with governance layered on top.

It should be a governed capital allocator that uses research competition to decide who earns the right to trade.

That distinction matters.

The first design is:

- "find signals and act"

The second design is:

- "earn permission, route trust, defend capital"

The second design is much more likely to survive contact with real markets.
